# Roadmap tradeoff review

When a new priority arrives, the product owner sees what the roadmap would have to give up to hold it: the capacity gap by team, the work that could move, the commitments and dependencies that move with it, and the questions still open. The owner decides.

*Workflow template · Product management · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/roadmap-tradeoff-review/)*

## The outcome

Someone asks for something new, and the answer stops being a feeling. The product owner has one page that says how much each team can take on, how much the request needs, how far short the plan falls, and which planned items could make room under rules written down in advance.

Nothing is hidden in the move. The page lists the commitments the displaced work carries, the later items that depend on it, and the dependencies of the request itself that nobody owns yet. It gives no date to anyone. The product owner takes it into the conversation with the person who asked, and into the one with the teams.

## Who starts the work, and when

The product owner starts it when a request, a customer need or a new dependency would add work to a planning window that is already full, and before anyone is told yes. A product manager or a founder who holds the product role can run it the same way. It is not for a milestone that has already slipped, which is [Schedule recovery options](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/schedule-recovery-options/), nor for scoring a new initiative by role and month for a portfolio owner, which is [Project intake and capacity review](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/project-intake-and-capacity-review/).

## What they supply

- The roadmap export: each item with its team, status, window, remaining effort, dependencies, goal and any commitment attached.
- Each team's capacity for the window.
- The company's goals in rank order.
- The request: who asked, the goal it serves, each team's effort estimate and the dependencies it brings.
- A few settings, such as whether committed or started work may be offered.

## What comes out

One page in a fixed order. It states the request and its effort by team, the capacity position of each team, a proposed set of displaced items with an alternative where one exists, the items that are not offered and why, the commitments touched, the dependencies left unresolved, a check against the goals, and the open questions with who can answer each. Displaced work gets no new date. It ends with the decision for the product owner.

See the worked example with invented data in [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md).

## What has to change for your company

Decide your planning window and your unit of effort. Set whether committed items and items already started may ever be offered, and the margin each team keeps free. Agree who ranks the goals and how often. Map your tracker's column names to the defaults in the template, or rename the columns in the export. Decide who may see the page before the product owner has decided.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
